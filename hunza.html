Hunza – Roof of the World

Ancient valleys, towering peaks, and a culture of remarkable warmth

Overview

The Hunza Valley, nestled in the Gilgit-Baltistan region of northern Pakistan, is widely regarded as one of the most spectacular destinations on Earth. Flanked by some of the world's highest mountain ranges — the Karakoram, Himalaya, and Hindu Kush — Hunza offers an incomparable landscape of glaciers, ancient forts, turquoise rivers, and terraced apricot orchards. For centuries it was an independent kingdom; today it is a premier destination for trekkers, mountaineers, and cultural travellers alike.

Region: Gilgit-Baltistan  |  Elevation: ~2,438 m  |  Best Season: April – October

Top Attractions

Baltit Fort

Perched dramatically on a rocky outcrop above the town of Karimabad, Baltit Fort is over 700 years old and was once the seat of the Mirs (rulers) of Hunza. Restored through a collaborative effort between the Aga Khan Trust for Culture and local communities, the fort is now a UNESCO Award-winning heritage site. It houses historical artefacts and offers breathtaking views of the valley and Rakaposhi peak.

Attabad Lake

Formed in 2010 after a massive landslide blocked the Hunza River, Attabad Lake is one of Pakistan's most striking natural wonders. Its vivid turquoise waters, set against towering brown and snow-capped peaks, create a surreal visual spectacle. Boat rides and jet-skiing on the lake have become major tourist attractions, and the surrounding area offers camping, cafés, and local craft markets.

Rakaposhi Viewpoint

Rakaposhi (7,788 m) is one of the most beautiful peaks in the Karakoram range and arguably the most photogenic mountain in Pakistan. The Rakaposhi Viewpoint near Minapin village offers a clear face-on view of the mountain's massive glaciated slopes. Sunrise and sunset here are extraordinary photographic opportunities.

Khunjerab Pass

At 4,693 metres above sea level, the Khunjerab Pass is the world's highest paved international border crossing, connecting Pakistan with China on the Karakoram Highway. Visiting here means standing on the literal roof of the world, with vast high-altitude plateaus, yaks grazing nearby, and snow even in midsummer.

Travel Tips

  • Travel via the Karakoram Highway (KKH) — one of the world's most scenic roads.
  • April is the best time for the legendary apricot blossom season — the valley turns white and pink.
  • Altitude sickness can affect visitors at Khunjerab Pass — ascend slowly and stay hydrated.
  • Try locally made mulberry and apricot jams — authentic souvenirs from Hunza.
  • Hire a local guide in Karimabad for trekking trails; many hidden viewpoints are unmarked.
  • Book accommodation early in summer; Hunza's limited hotel capacity fills up fast in July–August.
