Murree – The Queen of Hills

Pine forests, fresh air, and the magic of snowfall

Overview

Murree is Pakistan's most beloved and accessible hill station, perched at an elevation of approximately 2,291 metres (7,516 ft) in the Rawalpindi District of Punjab. Established as a summer retreat for British colonial officers in 1851, Murree retains much of its Victorian-era charm alongside breathtaking natural beauty. The town's cool pine-scented air and panoramic views of the surrounding mountains make it a year-round destination for families, nature lovers, and snow enthusiasts.

Province: Punjab  |  Elevation: 2,291 m  |  Best Season: Dec–Feb (snow) & Jun–Aug (summer)

Top Attractions

Mall Road

Mall Road is the lively commercial heart of Murree, running through the main bazaar with souvenir shops, restaurants, and cafés. During winter snowfall, Mall Road transforms into a magical scene as children play in the snow and hot chai vendors dot every corner. Street food highlights include corn on the cob, samosas, and freshly grilled corn with local spices.

Pindi Point

Pindi Point is a prominent viewpoint and the starting station of the Murree chairlift. The chairlift offers panoramic views of the surrounding hills, pine forests, and on a clear day, a glimpse of the Kashmir range. The area is also home to a small amusement park and food stalls, making it a popular family outing.

Patriata (New Murree)

Located 13 km from Murree town, Patriata — also known as New Murree — is a quieter and more scenic alternative. It features a chairlift and a cable car that ascend through lush forests and offer stunning views of the Himalayan foothills. Patriata is particularly beautiful in spring when wildflowers bloom across the hillsides.

Kashmiri Bazaar

Kashmiri Bazaar in the lower part of Murree is famous for its traditional Kashmiri shawls, woollenware, wooden crafts, and dried fruits. It is the best place to pick up authentic souvenirs including hand-embroidered fabrics and locally harvested pine nuts and walnuts.

Travel Tips

  • Book accommodation well in advance for December–January as rooms sell out weeks ahead.
  • Pack warm layers — temperatures can drop below freezing at night even in autumn.
  • Visit on weekdays to avoid heavy weekend traffic on the Murree Expressway.
  • Try the Kashmiri noon chai (pink salt tea) — a warming local speciality.
  • If visiting in snow season, ensure your vehicle has snow chains or four-wheel drive.
